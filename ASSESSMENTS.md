Daily quizzes and interview practice are part of the planned curriculum from Day 1, but administration requires the learner's explicit opt-in for each activity.

## Visual and interactive assessment design

- In ChatGPT sessions, prefer interactive one-question-at-a-time checks or compact controls when they improve participation. Let the learner attempt before revealing answers.
- In repository Markdown, use clear numbered questions, tables, scenario prompts and checklists. These are static documents, not embedded interactive widgets.
- Include visual questions where useful: interpret a commit graph, trace a file-state diagram, predict a flowchart outcome or diagnose a terminal output.
- Use consistent color cues, but make every question answerable without color.
- For output-prediction questions, label the terminal state as a scenario; never imply it came from the learner's machine.
- Track correctness, reasoning, safety judgment and ability to apply the concept. A quiz score alone does not establish practical verification.


## Assessment placement and evidence separation

- Teach the relevant concept before presenting its quiz or interview prompts; do not place a course checkpoint table ahead of the lesson.
- Keep questions and learner attempts in the learning/assessment flow. Record scores, feedback, topic gaps and retest evidence in this file and the relevant progress records.
- A quiz being displayed is not an attempt; an answer being submitted is not automatically practical verification.


## Lesson overview vs lesson assessment vs verification

Keep the lesson's opening overview separate from the teaching and from the final evidence record:

- **Before teaching — Lesson Overview:** objectives, prerequisites, scope, practice/deliverable and verification criteria (the standard the learner is expected to meet).
- **During teaching — Lesson / Study Material:** topic-by-topic explanations, demonstrations, practice prompts and quiz/interview questions. Begin this section under a distinct `Lesson / Study Material` heading, followed by `Topic 1`, `Topic 2`, etc.
- **After the lesson — Verification Record:** learner responses, scores, feedback, practical evidence, status and next action. Record these in this file and the course tracking files, not in the overview.

A verification criterion is not a result. A question being shown is not an attempt; an attempt is not automatically a pass; a quiz score alone does not prove practical competence.


## Lesson opening presentation — no pre-lesson form

Use the canonical inline lesson structure:
- Start with **Part A — Pre-Lesson** as a normal visible heading, followed by concise topic/session, time (if useful), objectives, prerequisites, scope and prospective verification criteria.
- Continue immediately with **Part B — Lesson / Study Material** and the actual topic teaching.
- Do not turn Part A into a form, radio-group questionnaire, collapsed panel, separate dashboard or progress report. No learner input is required before the lesson starts.
- Keep learner answers, scores, feedback and evidence in the assessment/progress records after the learner has attempted the work.
- Do not show course-wide status or installation/evidence audits in the opening lesson section.


## Learner opt-in is required before administering any assessment

Assessment plans in this file are curriculum records, not permission to administer them automatically.

- Do not present a quiz, test, interview question, mock interview, knowledge check, scored assessment or assessment exercise until the learner explicitly requests that specific activity.
- Do not present practical tasks, command-running exercises, homework or deliverables until the learner explicitly requests practical work.
- Teach the relevant concepts thoroughly first. "Continue" and "next topic" are not consent to assess.
- If the learner asks to start assessment/practical work before the concepts have been adequately taught, remind them that they asked to learn the concepts first and ask whether they explicitly want to begin anyway. Wait for confirmation.
- A request to start a quiz authorises the quiz, not practical work or an interview. Treat each activity separately.
- Keep assessment templates and curriculum coverage intact; defer actual administration until opt-in.

## Learner-controlled practice and assessment — mandatory consent gate

**Default mode is concept teaching only.** Do not give or assign quizzes, tests, assessments, interview questions, mock interviews, practicals, command-running tasks, homework, challenges, exercises or deliverables unless the learner explicitly asks to begin that specific activity.

- Teach concepts thoroughly first, one concept at a time. Do not interpret "continue", "next topic", moving to another day/module, or asking for more explanation as permission to assess or assign practical work.
- Do not insert a "quick check", knowledge check, prediction question, interview prompt or one-question quiz during concept teaching. Explanations and instructor-led demonstrations are allowed; learner execution is not requested by default.
- The learner may ask conceptual questions at any time. You may include worked examples and model-answer explanations as part of teaching when useful, without turning them into an assessment.
- If the learner tries to move ahead to a later day/module while previously planned quizzes or assessments remain incomplete, gently remind them that those activities are still pending and ask whether they want to continue concept learning or explicitly begin a particular assessment. Do not start it without clear permission.
- If the learner explicitly asks to begin one activity (for example, "quiz me now" or "start the practical"), that permission applies only to that activity. It does not authorise other activity types.
- If the learner requests study mode, questions with answers, or model answers, provide them as requested; do not misrepresent this as an attempted assessment.
- Preserve all planned quizzes, assessments, practicals and deliverables in the curriculum. Defer their administration until explicit opt-in; do not delete or silently skip them.
- Record only activities actually authorised and undertaken. A demonstrated command is not learner execution. Do not mark Practised or Verified without learner evidence and review.

**Reminder rule:** if the learner advances to a later day or lesson without completing previously deferred quizzes/assessments/practicals, remind them of the pending activities before proceeding. Respect their choice if they explicitly choose to continue learning concepts first.

This rule overrides any older wording that schedules automatic quizzes, interview practice, exercises, practicals or deliverables at the end of a lesson.
